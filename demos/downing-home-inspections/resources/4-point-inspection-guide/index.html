Florida 4-point inspection guide

What insurers look for in the roof, electrical, plumbing and HVAC, and the issues that most often cause trouble.

What a 4-point inspection is

A 4-point inspection is a short report on the four systems insurers worry about most in older homes: the roof, electrical, plumbing and HVAC. It records the type, age and condition of each, with photos, so the insurance company can decide whether to write or renew a policy.

It is not a full home inspection. It doesn't cover structure, appliances, windows or most of what a buyer needs to know, which is why buyers usually get both.

When you'll need one

Insurers usually ask for a 4-point once a home reaches a certain age, often somewhere between 20 and 30+ years. Each carrier sets its own cutoff, so your agent will tell you if yours needs one.

What gets checked

  • Roof: covering type, age, estimated remaining life, and any leaks or damage. Photos of each slope.
  • Electrical: panel brand and age, amperage, wiring type and any hazards. Photos with the panel cover off.
  • Plumbing: pipe materials, water heater age and condition, and any visible leaks.
  • HVAC: system age, whether it works, and its general condition.

Common reasons a home has trouble

Every insurer has its own rules, but these come up often in Central Florida homes:

  • An older roof with little remaining life, or signs of leaks.
  • Certain electrical panels, such as Federal Pacific, Zinsco and some other older brands, which many insurers won't accept.
  • Aluminum branch wiring or cloth-insulated wiring, common in homes from the 1960s and '70s.
  • Polybutylene supply pipes, a gray plastic pipe used from the late 1970s into the '90s. Many insurers won't accept it.
  • An old water heater or active leaks.

If something is flagged, the insurer may decline coverage or require a repair within a set time. Finding out early gives you time to get quotes, especially if you're buying.

How to get ready

  • Clear space around the electrical panel, water heater and air handler.
  • Gather the dates of any roof, electrical, plumbing or HVAC updates, plus permits or receipts if you have them.
  • The permit history for the address can confirm those dates. Find your city or county's permit search.

Questions

Who can do a 4-point?

Insurers require the form to be completed and signed by a Florida-licensed professional, such as a licensed home inspector.

What if my home doesn't pass?

There's no pass or fail on the form itself, but the insurer may decline coverage or ask for repairs. Fix the issue, then have the inspector update the report.

Should I get a wind mitigation inspection too?

Usually, yes. The wind mitigation form can lower your premium, and booking both together saves time.
