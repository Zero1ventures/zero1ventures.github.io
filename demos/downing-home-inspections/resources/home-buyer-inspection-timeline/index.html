Your Florida inspection period, day by day

A practical plan for the inspection period in a Florida purchase contract, so nothing gets rushed before your deadline.

  1. Before you make an offer

    Check the flood zone, the permit history and the roof age. Ask your insurance agent for a rough quote on the home. A surprise here is easier to deal with before you're under contract.

  2. Day 0: contract is effective

    The inspection period usually starts on the effective date, when the last person signs. Find the length in your contract. On the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar "AS IS" contract, it's 15 days if the blank is left empty.

  3. Days 1–2: book inspections

    Call your inspector right away. Book the general home inspection plus anything else you need: wind mitigation and 4-point for insurance, WDO (termite), pool, or a sewer camera for older homes.

  4. Days 3–6: inspections happen

    Attend if you can, or at least join for the walk-through at the end. You'll learn more in 20 minutes on site than from any report.

  5. Days 5–9: read the report and follow up

    Go through the summary first. If the inspector recommends a specialist (roofer, electrician, plumber, HVAC), get them out now, not next week. Send the wind mitigation and 4-point to your insurance agent for a real quote.

  6. Days 9–13: decide

    Talk with your agent about asking for repairs, a credit or a lower price, or walking away. Leave time for the seller to respond.

  7. Before the period ends

    If you're going to cancel, written notice must reach the seller before the inspection period ends. Your agent usually handles this. Missing the deadline can put your deposit at risk.

  8. Before closing

    Do a final walk-through. Confirm agreed repairs were made and that nothing new has happened since the inspection.

Tips that save buyers the most trouble

  • Book your inspector the same day your offer is accepted. Good inspectors fill up, and specialist follow-ups take time.
  • Get insurance quotes during the inspection period, not after. Roof age and a 4-point can change what's available.
  • Check for open or expired permits. Find the permit search for the home's city or county.

This is general information, not legal advice. Your contract's terms control. Ask your real estate agent or a Florida real estate attorney about your specific deadlines.
